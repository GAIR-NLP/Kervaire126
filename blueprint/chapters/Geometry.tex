\chapter{Framed Manifolds of Kervaire Invariant One}
Browder's theorem turns the permanence of $h_6^2$ into a geometric
existence theorem. In dimensions at most $254$, a closed smooth stably
framed manifold of Kervaire invariant one exists precisely in a dimension
$2^{j+1}-2$, $j\ge1$, for which $h_j^2$ survives nontrivially in the
sphere Adams spectral sequence. This is the bounded comparison of
Theorem~\ref{sem:Literature:finite-browder}. The exclusion of \cite{HHR}
then applies in dimensions at least $254$.

Use the closed smooth stably framed manifolds and Kervaire invariant of
Definitions~\ref{sem:Literature:closed-framed-manifolds}
and~\ref{sem:Literature:kervaire-invariant}. The geometric comparisons
of Chapter~2 identify this invariant with the framing-induced Arf
invariant and relate Pontryagin--Thom classes to Adams detection.
We assume these identifications and the stated Browder comparison,
together with the inputs of the permanence theorem. The homotopy
statements use $2$-completed, connective spectra of finite type.

The dimension convention is the $4k+2$ convention in the introduction of
\cite{LWX126}. Thus the index in Browder's geometric criterion is a positive
integer: $j\geq 1$. The geometric criterion concerns positive dimensions congruent to $2$ modulo $4$.

\begin{theorem}[A framed manifold of Kervaire invariant one in dimension $126$]
\label{paper:1.1}
\source{AimPaper Theorem 1.1}
\level{coarse}
From \cite{LWX126}, Theorem 1.1.\par

\label{thm:main}
There exists a closed smooth stably framed manifold of dimension $126$
with Kervaire invariant one.

\uses{sem:Literature:finite-browder,paper:1.4}

\lean{KIP126.Geometry.kervaire_invariant_one_126}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{paper:1.4} gives nonzero survival of $h_6^2$. Apply
Browder's criterion, Theorem~\ref{sem:Literature:finite-browder}, with
$j=6$. Since $2^{6+1}-2=126$, it yields the required manifold.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dimensions admitting Kervaire invariant one]
\label{paper:1.2}
\source{AimPaper Corollary 1.2}
\level{coarse}
From \cite{LWX126}, Corollary 1.2.\par

A closed smooth stably framed $n$-manifold of Kervaire invariant one
exists if and only if
\[
 n\in\{2,6,14,30,62,126\}.
\]

\uses{paper:1.1,sem:Literature:finite-browder,sem:Literature:hhr-geometric,paper:1.4,sem:Literature:two-line-classical-inputs}

\lean{KIP126.Geometry.kervaire_invariant_one_iff}

\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $n\leq254$, the bounded Browder criterion,
Theorem~\ref{sem:Literature:finite-browder}, makes existence equivalent
to $n=2^{j+1}-2$ and nonzero survival of $h_j^2$, with $j\ge1$.
The low squares for $1\le j\le3$ survive by the classical inputs in
\cite{MayThesis1964}; $j=4,5$ are supplied by
\cite{MahowaldTangora,BJMtheta5}. Theorem~\ref{paper:1.4} supplies
$j=6$, while Theorem~\ref{sem:Literature:hhr-geometric} excludes
dimension $254$ and every higher dimension.

Precisely $1\le j\le6$ therefore occur. Evaluating $2^{j+1}-2$ at these six
indices gives $2,6,14,30,62,126$. Conversely each listed dimension is
supplied by its surviving square and Browder's criterion.
\end{proof}
