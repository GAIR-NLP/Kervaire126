\section{Supplement: model categories and topological comparison}

This section gives the model-category foundations of the stable
background. Starting from the ordinary topological category, we obtain
cofibers, suspension, triangles and tensor products from stable monoidal
models. We then describe synthetic hypercompletion and the ordinary
coproduct and homotopy-detection results. The arguments use the model and comparison hypotheses stated here,
together with the earlier Adams convergence and page comparisons when invoked.

To keep the ordinary and completed categories distinct, we continue to
write $S_{\mathrm{ord}}$ and $H\mathbb F_{2,\mathrm{ord}}$ in the
ordinary category. Replacing a localization by a locally small equivalent
category may be done without changing its objects.

\subsection*{Ordinary source and topological recognition}

\begin{definition}[Ordinary models and their topological comparisons]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-models}
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-source-recognition}
\level{fine}
Choose a locally small category $\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ord}}$ equivalent to the ordinary stable prespectrum localization, with the equivalence fixing objects. Fix a stable symmetric monoidal model, cofibers and coefficient spectrum for it. Likewise choose an ordinary synthetic category $\mathrm{Syn}_{\mathrm{ord}}$ with its stable model and analogue functor $\nu_{\mathrm{ord}}$. Retain the ordinary Hopf maps $\eta,\nu_3$, the pointed detector $D_{\mathrm{ord}}$ and its unit. The stable and tensor structures are constructed from these models; the classical cofiber choices follow from pretriangulation.
The following comparisons identify the ordinary stable category with
the stable homotopy category of prespectra.\par
Let $\mathcal P$ be the category of sequential prespectra of
based spaces. A based space is \emph{well-based} when its basepoint
inclusion is closed and has the homotopy extension property; a
prespectrum is well-based when every level is. Stable homotopy sets
of prespectra are formed from cubical based maps, their homotopies, and
the prespectrum structure maps.
Let $W_\pi$ consist of maps inducing bijections on these stable homotopy
sets in every integer degree, and write
$Q_\pi\colon\mathcal P\to\mathcal P[W_\pi^{-1}]$.
Fix the recognition equivalence
\[
 e\colon\mathcal P[W_\pi^{-1}]\simeq\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ord}}.
\]
It identifies the sphere prespectrum with $S_{\mathrm{ord}}$,
desuspension with $\Sigma^{-1}$,
and suspension of well-based prespectra with $\Sigma$.
Its natural stable-homotopy identifications respect the cubical
zero and addition and normalize the sphere identity to the positive
source generator.

The recognition also specifies a source coefficient prespectrum with
zeroth stable homotopy coordinate $\mathbb F_2$ and vanishing stable
homotopy in every nonzero degree. It identifies this prespectrum with
$H\mathbb F_{2,\mathrm{ord}}$, compatibly with its zeroth homotopy
coordinate and coefficient unit.
This source is the ordinary stable localization; completion uses the
separately specified $H\mathbb F_2$ localization.
Call a based space \emph{good} if it is compactly generated, well-based
and has based CW homotopy type. Assume also the following tensor and
cofiber comparisons. For suspension spectra of good based spaces, the
tensor agrees naturally with reduced smash and respects the sphere unit.
For a map of well-based
prespectra, the reduced mapping cone agrees with the chosen cofiber,
including its inclusion and boundary. For arbitrary prespectra, use a
specified good replacement and compare its diagonal smash naturally
with the tensor. The unit normalization on suspension spectra is specified by the
generator comparison above.

\subsection*{Stable structures and cofiber triangles}


\begin{definition}[Stable symmetric monoidal models]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:point-set-model}
\level{coarse}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-source-recognition}
Fix a pointed, complete, cocomplete, cofibrantly generated closed symmetric
monoidal model category $\mathrm{Sp}_{ps}$ and a functor
$L_{raw}\colon\mathrm{Sp}_{ps}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{raw}$ localizing its weak
equivalences. Here $\mathrm{Sp}_{raw}$ is the $H\mathbb F_2$ localization, initially
regarded only as a category. Assume that the derived suspension formed
from the functorial model cofiber is an equivalence.

The tensor satisfies the pushout-product axiom: the pushout-product of
two cofibrations is a cofibration and is trivial if either factor is
trivial. For every weak equivalence $q\colon Q\mathbf{1}\to\mathbf{1}$
with cofibrant source and every cofibrant $X$, the map $q\otimes 1_X$
is a weak equivalence. The following results derive the tensor
comparisons, preadditivity, integer shifts and pretriangulation of the
localized category from these assumptions.

For the hypercomplete synthetic category, assume a
pointed, complete, cocomplete, cofibrantly generated closed symmetric
monoidal model $\mathrm{Syn}_{ps}$ with the same pushout-product and unit
axioms, a weak-equivalence localization into that category, and equivalence
of its derived suspension. Retain the additional synthetic grading
functors, their composition, zero and integer-shift comparisons, full
faithfulness, the natural transformation $\lambda$, and the objectwise
shift--tensor comparisons. These grading and tensor comparisons refer to the structures derived
from this model.

\begin{theorem}[Functorial and derived model cofibers]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:model-cofiber}
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:derived-model-cofiber}
\level{fine}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:point-set-model}
Let $\mathcal P$ be a pointed cofibrantly generated model category with
the two small object arguments for its generating cofibrations and
generating trivial cofibrations. There are functorial
cofibration--trivial-fibration and trivial-cofibration--fibration
factorizations. In particular, there is a cofibrant replacement functor
$Q$ with a natural weak equivalence $QX\to X$.

For an arrow $f\colon X\to Y$, factor $Qf$ as a cofibration
$i_f\colon QX\to M_f$ followed by a trivial fibration $M_f\to QY$.
The pushout
\[
 C_m(f)=M_f\amalg_{QX}0
\]
defines a functor $C_m\colon\operatorname{Arrow}(\mathcal P)\to\mathcal P$.
Its values are cofibrant, and its inclusion $M_f\to C_m(f)$ is natural
in strict commutative squares. Applied to the two models and
followed by their localization functors, this gives the corresponding
classical and synthetic model cofiber functors.

Let $W$ denote the model weak equivalences, and let
$L\colon\mathcal P\to\mathcal H$ localize $W$.
The functor $C_m$ takes a commutative square whose two components lie
in $W$ to a weak equivalence. Let $W_{\rm arrow}$ denote this class of
squares and $Q_{\rm arrow}$ its localization functor. Then $C_m$ induces
\[
 \overline C_m\colon
 \operatorname{Arrow}(\mathcal P)[W_{\rm arrow}^{-1}]
 \longrightarrow\mathcal H,
 \qquad \overline C_m Q_{\rm arrow}\cong LC_m.
\]
There is a natural cofiber inclusion $LY\to LC_m(f)$, obtained by
inverting $L(M_f\to Y)$ and using the pushout inclusion. It descends
to the same localization of arrows. If $L$ preserves zero morphisms,
its composite with $L(f)$ is zero.
\lean{KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.cofibrationTrivialFibrationFactorization,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.trivialCofibrationFibrationFactorization,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.cofibrantReplacementFunctor,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.cofibrantReplacementCounit,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.cofiberFunctor,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.cofiberInclusion,KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Completed.spectrumModelCofiber,KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Completed.syntheticModelCofiber,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.cofiberFunctor_weakEquivalences,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.derivedCofiberFunctor,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.derivedCofiberFunctorComparison,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.localizedCofiberInclusion,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.derivedCofiberInclusion,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.localizedCofiberInclusion_comp}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The small object arguments give the two functorial factorizations.
Apply the first to $0\to X$ and then to $Qf$. Pushout functoriality
gives the cofiber maps and their identity and composition laws.
Cofibrations are closed under pushout and composition, so each
$C_m(f)$ is cofibrant.

Functorial replacement preserves componentwise weak equivalences by
two-out-of-three. For cofibrant arrows, factor a componentwise weak
equivalence through the relative cofibration construction and apply
the model lifting axioms and pushout stability of trivial cofibrations.
This proves homotopy invariance of the pushout cofiber. The universal
property of localization then gives the derived functor and the
descended inclusion. Naturality of the replacement comparison and
the zero composite in the pushout give the asserted zero composite.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Model suspension, boundary and derived shifts]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:model-suspension}
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:derived-model-suspension}
\level{fine}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:model-cofiber,sem:AdamsSynthetic:derived-model-cofiber}
In the setting of the cofiber construction above, define
$\Sigma_m X=C_m(X\to0)$. The strict square from
$f\colon X\to Y$ to $X\to0$, with components $1_X$ and $Y\to0$,
induces a natural map
\[
 \partial_f\colon C_m(f)\longrightarrow\Sigma_m X.
\]
Both suspension and this boundary use the same functorial replacement
and pushout construction as $C_m$.

The model suspension preserves weak equivalences and induces a functor
$\overline\Sigma_m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H$ with
$\overline\Sigma_m L\cong L\Sigma_m$. The boundary descends to
a natural transformation from $\overline C_m$ to the derived suspension
of the source, on the localization of point-set arrows.

The cofiber of an identity is zero after localization. For each
$f\colon X\to Y$, the localized cofiber sequence
\[
 LX\xrightarrow{L(f)}LY\longrightarrow LC_m(f)
 \xrightarrow{\partial_f}\overline\Sigma_m LX
 \xrightarrow{\overline\Sigma_m L(f)}\overline\Sigma_m LY
\]
has zero composites of consecutive arrows.

If this same derived suspension is an equivalence, it determines a
coherent integer shift on $\mathcal H$, whose shift by one is naturally
isomorphic to $\overline\Sigma_m$.
\lean{KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.suspensionFunctor,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.cofiberBoundary,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.derivedSuspensionFunctor,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.derivedCofiberBoundary,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.localizedCofiberIdentity_isZero,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.localizedCofiberInclusion_boundary_comp,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.localizedCofiberBoundary_suspension_comp,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.derivedSuspensionHasShift,KIP126.StableHomotopy.CofibrantGeneration.derivedSuspensionShiftOneIso}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Homotopy invariance of the cofiber applied to $X\to0$ gives suspension
invariance and hence its descent. The natural boundary descends by
the fully faithful precomposition functor for localization.
The point-set replacement of an identity is a weak equivalence and
a cofibration, so its pushout cofiber is weakly equivalent to zero.
Naturality gives the inclusion--boundary zero composite; the next
composite factors through the cofiber of an identity.

To construct all integer shifts from an autoequivalence, pass to a
skeleton, where it becomes a strict automorphism. Its integer powers
satisfy the group laws strictly. Transport this shift structure back
along the skeleton equivalence, including its associativity and unit
comparisons.
\end{proof}

Let $W$ be the weak equivalences of $\mathrm{Sp}_{ps}$ and let

Let $L\colon\mathcal P\to\mathcal H$ be the localization of a model
category at its weak equivalences $W$. Localizing point-set arrows at
componentwise weak equivalences gives a comparison functor
\[
 \overline{\operatorname{Arr}(L)}\colon
 \operatorname{Arr}(\mathcal P)[(W^{\mathrm{arr}})^{-1}]
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Arr}(\mathcal H),
\]
whose composite with the localization functor is naturally isomorphic
to $\operatorname{Arr}(L)$. This comparison is full.

equivalence. Its localized square is the required preimage.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pretriangulation from the stable model]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:model-pretriangulation}
\level{fine}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:model-cofiber-tr1-tr3,sem:AdamsSynthetic:derived-model-suspension}

let $L\colon\mathcal P\to\mathcal H$ be its weak-equivalence localization,

axiom hold with the model boundary and the negative of the shifted

\subsection*{Coproducts and homotopy detection}

\begin{theorem}[Small coproducts and their homotopy groups]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:sphere-coproducts}
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:homotopy-small-coproducts}
\level{fine}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-source-recognition}
The category $\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ord}}$ admits all small coproducts.
For every small family $(X_j)_{j\in J}$ and every $n\in\mathbb Z$,
the coproduct inclusions induce an isomorphism
\[
 \bigoplus_{j\in J}\pi_nX_j\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
       \pi_n\Bigl(\coprod_{j\in J}X_j\Bigr).
\]
Equivalently, the functor
$[S_{\mathrm{ord}}^n,-]$ preserves small coproducts, independently of
the chosen coproduct objects. In particular, if
$\pi_nS_{\mathrm{ord}}=0$ for $n\le-1$, then every small coproduct
of copies of $S_{\mathrm{ord}}$ has zero homotopy in those degrees.
\lean{KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Ordinary.smallSphereCoproduct,KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Ordinary.smallSphereCoproduct_homotopy_vanishes,KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Ordinary.smallSpectrumCoproducts,KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Ordinary.homotopy_preserves_smallCoproduct}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Represent the spectra in the prespectrum localization. Cubical
replacement supplies cofibrant representatives with $T_1$ levels;
their strict wedge is a coproduct in the localization with its original
branch maps. Compact cubical representatives meet only finitely many
non-basepoint branches, so every stable homotopy element factors through
a finite subwedge. The recognition comparison preserves these
inclusions and factorizations. For sphere summands, the sphere
prespectrum is already free on its based zero level and has the cubical
lifting property, giving the same construction directly.

Finite coproducts in the preadditive category are biproducts.
Finite support therefore makes the displayed map surjective, and the
projections onto individual branches make it injective. This identifies
the mapped coproduct diagram with the direct-sum diagram in abelian
groups and proves the assertion for every colimit choice. Applying it
to a family of spheres gives the vanishing statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Homotopy detects ordinary spectrum isomorphisms]
\label{sem:AdamsSynthetic:homotopy-detects-isomorphisms}
\level{fine}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-source-recognition,sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-models}
Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a map of ordinary spectra.
If $\pi_n(f)$ is bijective for every integer $n$, then $f$ is an
isomorphism in $\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathrm{ord}}$.
\lean{KIP126.AdamsSynthetic.Ordinary.isIso_of_homotopy_bijective}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\uses{sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-source-recognition,sem:AdamsSynthetic:ordinary-models}
Complete $f$ to its distinguished cofiber triangle.
Its homotopy exact sequence and the shift adjunction show that the
cofiber has zero homotopy in every integer degree. Choose a prespectrum
representative of that cofiber. Its constant based map to zero is a
stable equivalence and therefore an isomorphism after localization.
Recognition makes the cofiber zero. Exactness of the distinguished
triangle then makes $f$ an isomorphism.
\end{proof}
